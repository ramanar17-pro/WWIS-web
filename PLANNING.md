## Problem Statement
Wisdom Wealth International School (WWIS), Trichy, is rebuilding its website (wwistrichy.com) off WordPress entirely. The trigger: AI chatbots (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity) give **wrong facts** about WWIS when prospective parents ask about it — confirmed by the founder's own testing, not hypothetical. Increasingly, parents research and shortlist schools through AI search rather than by browsing sites directly, so wrong answers translate directly into lost consideration.

Investigation of the live site confirmed the root cause: it is **not** a crawler-access problem (the site is server-rendered HTML, robots.txt doesn't block AI bots). It's a **content gap** — tuition/fees, the admissions process, and staff credentials are simply not published anywhere on the site. AI has nothing accurate to draw from, so it fills the gap with stale third-party listings or hallucination.

## Demand Evidence
Founder personally tested AI chatbots by asking about WWIS and got confidently wrong facts back. This is the strongest kind of evidence available at this stage — not a survey, not a hypothetical, a repeatable, self-verified failure.

## Status Quo
- Current site: wwistrichy.com, WordPress, standard template (nav duplicated header/footer).
- Copyright year shows "2026" — a template bug signaling the site isn't actively maintained.
- No tuition/fee page. No documented admissions process. No staff credentials despite a "Team" section existing.
- robots.txt is generic WordPress default — doesn't block AI crawlers, but has no AI-specific handling either (no llms.txt, no special allowances).
- Net effect: even a perfectly crawlable site has nothing for AI to correctly cite.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
**Target user:** prospective parents using AI search/chat tools to research and shortlist schools for their children — the same parents who used to just Google "WWIS Trichy fees."

**Narrowest wedge:** a small set of canonical facts (tuition, admissions process, curriculum detail per program, staff credentials, contact/location) published as clean, structured content — the seed of the new site, not the whole rebuild.

## Constraints
- Full rebuild off WordPress — static site, Astro/Next.js-class stack (founder's stated preference).
- This design session scopes the **AI-visibility/facts foundation only** — it is the starting point of the rebuild, not the full site (blog, events, gallery, career sections come later).
- Non-technical school staff will eventually need to update facts (tuition changes yearly, staff turn over) — the architecture has to survive that, not just the initial build.
- There is a sponsor at WWIS (principal/admissions/comms) who needs to see proof this works — no demo artifact identified yet.

## Premises
1. The core gap is MISSING facts (tuition, admissions, staff, curriculum detail) — not JS/crawler access, which is already fine. — **agreed**
2. llms.txt is not the fix — major AI crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google's AI crawlers) don't read it. Clean HTML + schema.org structured data + off-site accuracy is what actually moves AI answers. — **agreed**
3. This needs a content owner at WWIS (not just a dev), since facts like tuition and policies go stale and someone has to keep them current quarterly. — **agreed**

## Landscape Findings (GEO / AI-search visibility, August 2026)
- GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) research indicates the discipline is ~80% strategic (positioning, off-site brand presence) and ~20% technical.
- Content freshness matters: AI citation rates drop sharply once content is >3 months stale — this validates premise #3 (need an ongoing owner, not a one-time build).
- AI systems weight brand/entity mentions across the whole web, not just the owned site — off-site accuracy (directories, Google Business Profile, review sites) matters alongside on-site content.
- llms.txt has no official adoption by GPTBot, ClaudeBot, or Google's AI crawlers as of this research — confirmed, informs premise #2.
- Sources: [Writer — GEO/AEO/SEO 2026](https://writer.com/blog/geo-aeo-optimization/), [Yoast — What AI gets wrong about your site](https://yoast.com/what-ai-gets-wrong-about-your-site-llms-txt/), [Semrush — What Is LLMs.txt](https://www.semrush.com/blog/llms-txt/)

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Facts-First MVP (minimal viable)
Static site (Astro/Next.js), 5-6 hardcoded pages: home, tuition, admissions, curriculum, staff, contact. Manual schema.org JSON-LD per page.
- Effort: ~2-4 days. Risk: Low.
- Pros: ships fastest, fastest before/after proof point.
- Cons: content lives inside page templates — every future fact update means touching code, which won't survive staff turnover or a non-technical content owner.

### Approach B: Structured Content Core (ideal architecture) — CHOSEN
Single canonical facts source using **Astro Content Collections** (Markdown files with YAML frontmatter, one per topic: tuition, admissions, staff, curriculum, contact) — the format decision is made here, not deferred; Content Collections is the native Astro pattern and gives typed, validated content out of the box. Each collection generates both the human-readable page AND schema.org JSON-LD automatically from the same source. Includes a `lastUpdated` frontmatter field per entry (surfaced on-page as a freshness signal) and a quarterly content-review cadence.
- Effort: ~1.5-2.5 weeks for the full pattern across all 5 topics; ~3-4 days if scoped to tuition only first (see Recommended Approach below).
- Risk: Low-Medium — the pattern itself is standard Astro usage, the risk is organizational (getting real content in time), not technical.
- Pros: one edit updates both the human page and the machine-readable markup — matches the "needs an ongoing content owner" premise directly. The architecture *supports* a future non-technical editing layer (a lightweight headless CMS like Decap CMS can point at the same Content Collections), but plain Markdown files edited via git are realistically a developer/technical-comfort workflow, not a genuinely non-technical one — see Open Questions.
- Cons: more upfront setup than A; non-technical editing still requires a CMS decision (open question below) before staff can self-serve.
- Reuses: since this is a from-scratch build (no WordPress legacy), the structured-source pattern costs almost nothing extra versus hardcoding — no migration debt to work around.

### Approach C: Answer-Engine-First (creative/lateral)
Content authored directly in the Q&A shape parents actually use with AI ("What's tuition at WWIS Trichy?"), with FAQPage schema, plus a public shareable fact-sheet page. Prioritizes the highest-leverage AI-visibility pages over full completeness.
- Effort: ~3-5 days. Risk: Low.
- Pros: sharpest single demo artifact for the sponsor; matches GEO research finding that AI mirrors how people phrase questions.
- Cons: narrower than B — doesn't solve the long-term content-freshness/ownership problem on its own.

## Recommended Approach
**Approach B — Structured Content Core — scoped to tuition first.** Since this is a ground-up rebuild with no WordPress constraints, doing the structured-content-as-source-of-truth pattern now is nearly free compared to hardcoding pages (Approach A), and it's the only approach that structurally satisfies premise #3 (an ongoing content owner needs to be able to update facts without breaking the machine-readable output).

This reconciles with the Narrowest Wedge principle above (which describes a single-topic seed, not a full rebuild) by building the *pattern* once and proving it end-to-end on **one topic — tuition** — before extending it to admissions, curriculum, and staff. Tuition is the topic prospective parents ask AI about most, so it's both the highest-leverage first fact and the cheapest way to validate the architecture (schema shape, JSON-LD generation, freshness timestamp) before committing to the same shape for the other four topics. Approach C's Q&A framing is folded into B's page templates rather than treated as a separate track — it costs nothing extra to write facts in Q&A-matching language once the content model exists.

## Content Schema Sketch
Minimal frontmatter fields per topic — enough to ground engineering estimates and the content-gathering assignment in the same shape. Refine once real content is gathered (see Dependencies). Each topic also lists the anchor schema.org type its JSON-LD serializes to, so the "generates JSON-LD automatically" claim in Approach B has a concrete target rather than being invented at implementation time.

**Tuition** (`src/content/tuition/*.md`) → schema.org: `Offer` nested under the site's `EducationalOrganization`. Fields: `programName`, `gradeRange`, `annualFee` (or `feeRange` if exact figures aren't approved for publishing — see Dependencies), `currency`, `additionalFees` (list: admission fee, transport, etc.), `academicYear`, `lastUpdated`.

**Admissions** (`src/content/admissions/*.md`) → schema.org: `HowTo` (steps as `HowToStep`). Fields: `stepNumber`, `stepTitle`, `description`, `requiredDocuments` (list), `deadline`, `contactEmail`, `lastUpdated`.

**Staff** (`src/content/staff/*.md`) → schema.org: `Person`. Fields: `name`, `role`, `credentials` (list), `bio`, `photoUrl`, `department`, `lastUpdated`.

**Curriculum** (`src/content/curriculum/*.md`) → schema.org: `Course` nested under `EducationalOrganization`. Fields: `programName` (iPlay/iDiscover/iLead), `gradeRange`, `curriculumBoard` (e.g. Cambridge), `keySkills` (list), `summary`, `lastUpdated`.

**Contact/Location** (`src/content/contact/*.md`) → schema.org: `EducationalOrganization` (address/contact fields directly on the root entity). Fields: `campusName`, `address`, `phone`, `email`, `mapUrl`, `officeHours`, `lastUpdated`.

## Open Questions
- Content-editing solution for non-technical staff: plain content files (Markdown/YAML in the repo, edited via GitHub) vs. a lightweight headless CMS (Decap CMS, Sanity, etc.)? Depends on who at WWIS will actually maintain this and their comfort with git.
- Hosting/deploy target: Vercel, Netlify, or Cloudflare Pages?
- DNS/domain cutover plan: how and when does wwistrichy.com point away from WordPress to the new static site? (Distribution Plan marks this TBD pending the answer here.)
- Full site scope timing: when do the remaining sections (blog, events, gallery, career) get rebuilt — same effort, later phase, or deferred indefinitely?
- Who owns quarterly content review once shipped? Same person as the off-site directory/GBP audit, or someone else?
- Does WWIS have more than one campus, curriculum board, or a language requirement (e.g. Tamil alongside English) that the content schema above needs to account for? Sketch above assumes single-campus, single-board (Cambridge) until confirmed otherwise.

## Success Criteria
- A fixed, repeatable set of test queries — the ones that originally surfaced wrong facts, e.g. "What is tuition at WWIS Trichy?", "How do I apply to WWIS Trichy?", "What curriculum does WWIS Trichy follow?" — logged with today's (wrong) answers as the baseline.
- Re-running that same query set against the three tools named in the Problem Statement (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity) — split by whichever mode each supports: browsing-enabled where available, base-model (no live browsing) otherwise — at two checkpoints — **2 weeks** and **6 weeks** post-launch — to account for the fact that base models relying on stale training data may not reflect published changes on any near-term timeline, while browsing-enabled tools should update faster. Success is measured against the browsing-enabled checkpoint at 2 weeks at minimum; base-model correction is a longer-horizon, lower-confidence goal.
- Core facts pages (starting with tuition) live with schema.org JSON-LD generated from the structured content source (not hand-duplicated), and the markup **passes both Google's Rich Results Test and the general schema.org Markup Validator (validator.schema.org) with zero errors** — Rich Results Test alone only confirms eligibility for Google's rich-result-eligible types, not general schema.org correctness, so the second check closes that gap.
- Sponsor has the before/after query-answer log as a concrete artifact to evaluate.

## Off-Site Accuracy
The Landscape Findings above are explicit that GEO is ~80% strategic/off-site and only ~20% technical — AI systems weight brand mentions across the whole web, not just the owned site. On-site structured content (Approach B) addresses the 20%. Scoped small for this phase:
- Audit and correct WWIS's listing on the 3-5 highest-traffic third-party sources (Google Business Profile, any education directory listings, review sites) for the same facts being fixed on-site (tuition, address, curriculum).
- This is a content-accuracy task, not a build task — it can run in parallel with the Approach B build and doesn't block it.

## Dependencies
- **Real content is the actual blocker, not code**: accurate current tuition figures, the real admissions process steps, and staff bios/credentials need to be sourced from whoever owns that information at WWIS. This has to happen before or alongside the build — no amount of good architecture fixes wrong or missing source facts.
- Sponsor alignment on which facts are safe to publish publicly. **Fallback if exact tuition figures aren't approved for publication**: publish a fee range or "starting from" figure per program rather than blocking the whole tuition page on a binary yes/no — the schema sketch above already supports this via `feeRange` as an alternative to `annualFee`.
